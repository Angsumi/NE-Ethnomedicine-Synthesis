## 4. How to make this a top-quality meta-analysis

### A. Make the literature search reproducible (reviewers check this first)
1. **Register a protocol** on OSF before final screening. It should state the research questions, search strategy, eligibility criteria and analysis plan.
2. **Search databases:** Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, CAB Abstracts, J-Gate.
3. **Search Indian and grey literature:** CSIR-NOPR (*IJTK*, *IJNPR*, *NPR*), Shodhganga theses, and Google Scholar (first 300 hits per string).
4. **Snowball** through the reference lists of the reviews (Jha & Smith-Hall 2023; Barbhuiya 2022; the Arunachal 14-tribe review; Begum 2024).
5. **Search terms:** a fixed string combining the concepts (ethnomedic\* OR ethnobotan\* OR "traditional medicine" OR "folk medicine") AND (each state name OR each district OR each tribe name). Report the exact strings, dates and number of hits per database.
6. **PRISMA 2020:** a flow diagram plus the checklist, listing the reason for every exclusion (including "full text not retrieved" for S13, S20 and S32).
7. **Two independent screeners** for titles, abstracts and full texts, with Cohen's κ reported. Double-extract a 20% sample and report the agreement.

### B. Eligibility criteria (write them down and apply them mechanically)
- **Include:** primary field surveys in the 7 states that name the community, identify plants to species, and report human medicinal use.
- **Exclude:** reviews used as data (S15, S32, X02), preprints (keep for sensitivity only), and studies without species-level identification.
- **Duplicate publications:** when the same field data appears in more than one paper, keep the most complete version.
- **Handle separately:** veterinary uses, food-only plants, and non-ST communities (analyse them as a stratum, not dropped silently).

### C. Appraise the quality of every study
Score each study against the ConSEFS standards (Heinrich et al. 2018, *J. Ethnopharmacol.* 211:329):
- vouchers deposited, with the herbarium named
- number and selection of informants reported
- prior informed consent or ethics approval
- taxonomic authority checked
- methods replicable
- quantitative indices given

Use the score to:
1. describe how reliable the evidence base is;
2. run sensitivity analyses that exclude low-score studies;
3. weight the citation-based indices.

### D. Standardise the data properly
1. **Taxonomy:** resolve every name against World Flora Online or POWO (R: `WorldFlora`, `rWCVP`), merge synonyms, and record native or introduced status from POWO distributions. This step alone will change species counts by roughly 5–10%.
2. **Use categories:** double-code ailments to the Economic Botany Data Collection Standard (Cook 1995) and map them to ICD-11 chapters. ICD-11 is what pharmacology reviewers expect.
3. **Controlled vocabularies:** use fixed lists for plant part, preparation, route and life form.
4. **Conservation status:** add IUCN Red List status (via the API) and NE endemism.
5. **Unit of analysis:** count *use reports*, i.e. species × use category × community × study, not raw rows.

### E. Analyses that go beyond a descriptive paper (these make it "best")

| Question | Method |
|---|---|
| How complete is the documented pharmacopoeia? | Species accumulation and rarefaction plus a Chao estimator (`iNEXT`) per state. This gives an estimate of how many medicinal species remain undocumented. |
| Which plants are a shared NE "core pharmacopoeia"? | Number of independent communities and studies citing a species for the same category (convergent use is evidence of efficacy). |
| Does medicinal knowledge follow language or geography? | Jaccard similarity between communities, then NMDS plus Mantel / partial-Mantel / PERMANOVA against geographic distance, language family and physiographic zone. **This is a strong novel angle for NE India.** |
| Which families are over- or under-used? | Moerman regression residuals, or Bayesian binomial (Weckerle et al. 2011), against regional flora totals per family. |
| Is medicinal use phylogenetically clustered? | Build a phylogeny with V.PhyloMaker2, compute the D statistic, and find "hot nodes" (Saslis-Lagoudakis et al. 2012, *PNAS*). This points to bioprospecting candidates. |
| How do communities share plants? | A bipartite network of communities and plants, with modularity and connector species. |
| Which uses are pharmacologically supported? | For the top consensus species × use, a systematic PubMed/Scopus check for in vitro, in vivo and clinical evidence, giving a "validated / untested" matrix. Flag toxic taxa (*Aconitum*, *Datura*, *Gloriosa*). |
| What needs conservation priority? | IUCN status + endemism + destructive harvest (root, bark, whole plant), combined into a priority index. |
| Where is the research bias? | A GIS map of study districts against tribe population and forest cover, plus publication trends by decade. |

### F. Reporting and transparency
- **Release the data and code:** deposit the cleaned dataset with a DOI on Zenodo, link the GitHub pipeline, and include a data dictionary. A companion *data paper* (e.g. *Scientific Data* or *Biodiversity Data Journal*) is realistic.
- **Report heterogeneity and bias openly:** the over-representation of Assam and Arunachal, the very different sample sizes across studies, and the fact that informant-level indices (UV, ICF, FL) **cannot be pooled** across studies.
- **Ethics:** mention the Biological Diversity Act 2002 and the Nagoya Protocol, acknowledge the communities, and don't republish knowledge that the source studies marked as sensitive.

### G. Target journals
In order of ambition: *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* (requires the quality appraisal and pharmacological validation), *Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine*, *Frontiers in Pharmacology* (Ethnopharmacology section), *Ethnobotany Research and Applications*, *Phytomedicine Plus* (where the West Bengal paper appeared).

## 5. Suggested order of work
1. Re-run the search systematically (A) and screen with a second reviewer. This also confirms the candidate list in section 3.
2. Extract the new studies, prioritising Nagaland, Manipur, the Patkai belt, Tripura (Halam, Jamatia, Reang) and the Assam non-ST communities.
3. Resolve taxonomy with WFO/POWO and double-code the use categories.
4. Score study quality.
5. Run the section E analyses and the sensitivity analyses.
6. Write the paper and release the data.
